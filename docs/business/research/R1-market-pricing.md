# R1 - Market pricing and buyer research: motion graphics, explainer and product video

Researched 2026-11-07 (about 30 searches and 6 page fetches). Almost every price below comes from vendor blogs and pricing pages, which have a stake in the numbers. Independent surveys are rare. Each claim carries its URL and the date the source gave. Where sources disagree there is a **Confidence** note. Where I found nothing, the file says so.

## 0. How to read this

- "Vendor" means the source sells video or AI video tools. Treat its figures as directional.
- Prices are USD unless stated.
- Gaps (not found): who decides per segment with hard data; a Motion Design School 2025 rate survey; a Wyzowl cost-barrier chart; real-estate and education segment budgets; Vucko pricing; any 2027 forecast.

## 3. What buyers want, and what makes them happy or unhappy

### Outcomes buyers pay for
- **Landing page conversion.** "Up to 80%" lift from an explainer is the most repeated claim (Wyzowl 2026, repeated by many blogs; one claim, not independent proof). Other claims: pages with embedded demo video 4.8% vs 2.9% (about 65%); 30-90 s product video pages convert 30%+ higher (Wistia 2026 via Dupple). None verified against originals. https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-study-2000-pages-tested-2026
- **Ad performance.** Agency sources say brands testing 20+ new ads a month see about 65% higher ROAS than those testing under 10 (Common Thread Collective, 170+ brands, second-hand). Meta internal test (reported): 25 diverse creatives beat 5x5, +17% conversions at -16% cost. https://segwise.ai/blog/meta-andromeda-update-creative-strategy-2026 . Only 5-10% of tested creatives become winners (agency analysis in the UGC pricing results). **Confidence: low**, vendor sourced.
- **Launch buzz and investor/recruiting signal.** Startups spend on hype videos to stand out, recruit and look established (The Star, 2026-05-31).
- **General market health.** Wyzowl State of Video Marketing 2026 (12th edition, 266 respondents, late 2025): 91% of businesses use video; 92% plan same or more spend; 82% report good ROI (down from 93%); 63% of video marketers have used AI to create or edit video (up from 51%); top reasons for not using video: "not needed" and "too expensive" (24% each), then no time (19%). https://wyzowl.com/sovm-results-2025/ and secondary summaries (https://piktochart.com/blog/video-marketing-statistics-2026/ ). **Confidence:** the Wyzowl page returned 403 to my fetch; figures are from secondary summaries. One source reports 89% adoption, another 91%. A Wyzowl-2025 figure of 75% AI use conflicts with the 63% above (probably different questions).

### What makes buyers happy (evidence is thin and mostly vendor guidance)
- A written scope that names aspect ratios, resolution, captions, silent version and editable files (webdew SOW pages and buyer guides found by search).
- A clear brief and one decision-maker: vendors blame conflicting feedback from several stakeholders for revision loops (BigButton, Breadnbeyond pieces).
- Speed: Motion the Agency sells 24-48 h turnaround and a free 10 s sample as differentiators (2026-09-04).
- Real stories and quality that AI cannot match, for startups (Nen Creative, The Star, 2026-05-31).

### Top complaints (anecdotal)
- Missed deadlines despite "unlimited revisions" contracts (GoodFirms and Trustpilot reviews, one vendor; two anecdotes, not a sample).
- Hidden costs and poor communication (review-reading guide).
- Generic or low-quality AI content lowers trust: 32% of consumers say so (Klaviyo, 7,998 consumers, 2026, second-hand).
- **No representative buyer survey of explainer vendors was found.** An agency-side survey (not video-specific) said 48.3% blame client schedules for missed deadlines vs 13.8% internal inefficiency (via strategyonline.ca).

## 4. Standard deliverables package for a $2k+ client

Source base: vendor scope-of-work pages (webdew), Motion the Agency, Yum Yum. No industry standard document exists, so this is a synthesis.

| Item | Common practice | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Script and storyboard | Included at studio tier; storyboard revisions often unlimited (Motion) | Yum Yum recommends checking script, storyboard, illustration, animation, voiceover, sound, PM, revisions and final versions are in the quote |
| Revisions | 2 rounds is the common default (some 2-3); extra rounds cost extra; "unlimited" often has conditions | https://support.webdew.com/kb/services/explainer-video-scope-of-work |
| Turnaround | 4-6 weeks typical (24-30 business days quoted); Yum Yum median 7 weeks; freelancer 60-90 s in 5-10 business days; Motion 24-48 h on its fixed plan; clock excludes client review wait | webdew SOW; Yum Yum; Motion |
| Master file | MP4 full HD 16:9 | webdew SOW |
| Other aspect ratios | Often a paid add-on or higher package. Example: $150 per aspect-ratio variation | webdew SOW |
| Captions / SRT | Often priced separately (example: $30 for English captions) | webdew SOW |
| Cut-downs | Wyzowl includes social cutdowns only in the 90 s ($9,000) package | Motion the Agency, 2026-09-04 |
| Source / project files | Usually an extra cost or on request; regulated-industry vendors deliver editable files for compliance edits | webdew; Explainify fintech page |
| Voice-over | Professional VO usually included at studio tier | Motion, Yum Yum |
| Music licence | **No dedicated source found.** Inference: licensed stock music is included and should be stated | - |
| Thumbnails | **No source found** | - |
| Copyright | Transfer on final payment (stated by at least one fintech studio) | https://explainify.com/lp/animated-video-production-fintech/ |

**Opportunity:** the market treats 4:5, 9:16, captions and cut-downs as paid extras. Bundling 16:9, 1:1, 9:16, 4:5, SRT, and 6/15/30 s cut-downs by default is a differentiator at no big cost for a code-driven pipeline (inference).

## 5. How AI is changing the market (2025-2026)

- **Low end is commoditised.** AI-made explainers quoted at $497-$1,500 vs $2,500-$7,500 custom (Knowlify, vendor). AI ad avatars about $11 per video (Arcads Starter, $110/mo for 10 videos). Vendor AI blogs claim demos at $15-$79 each; ignore as marketing. Yum Yum: AI/template tools suit low-stakes work but not brand-specific style, careful scripts, multiple feedback rounds, or investor-facing products (2026-08-06).
- **Pricing pressure is real in agencies.** Digiday (indie agencies, 2026): clients want more for the same spend and test AI output in meetings; some agencies bill AI "tokens", some absorb them. https://digiday.com/marketing/as-ai-alters-cost-of-creative-indie-agencies-review-how-they-charge-clients/ . No standard model yet.
- **What still commands premium:** strategy and craft, outcome-based pricing, brand fidelity, real customer stories and human presence. Nen Creative (video studio for startups) lost one deal to AI on cost but says AI cannot match its quality and real-story demand is rising (The Star, 2026-05-31). Many startups avoid cheap AI video because it looks unprofessional (same article).
- **Buyer attitudes (consumers, not buyers):** Animoto Jan 2026 (450+ US consumers and marketers): 83% say they can spot AI video, 36% say it lowers brand trust, over a third trust it as much as human-made. IAB/Sonata: 82% of ad execs think Gen Z/Millennials feel positive about AI ads vs 45% of consumers. eMarketer: enthusiasm for AI content fell from 60% (2023) to 26% (2025). Pattern: people reject hidden or generic AI, and AI-generated people hurt trust more than AI scenes (VCU study, second-hand). https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260121875037/en/83-of-Consumers-Can-Spot-AI-Videos-36-Say-It-Lowers-Brand-Trust-According-to-Animotos-New-Report . **Confidence: low to medium**; the "83%" is described differently by different outlets.
- **Studios using AI:** I found generic vendor claims (freelancers charging 40-80% more with AI skills, 70% lower costs) but no independent data. Treat as unverified.

**Implication:** an AI-assisted code-based studio is not the same as "AI video". Motion graphics rendered from HTML/GSAP stay vector-sharp and brand-exact, and avoid the AI-people trust penalty (inference).

## 7. Top 10 takeaways for a new AI-powered studio

1. **The money is in the $5,000-$15,000 band for a 60 s custom B2B/SaaS video.** Real quotes: median $9,680 across 61 quotes (Yum Yum, July 2026); SaaS launch video $5,000-$15,000 (Flowjam, Aug 2026). A $300 gig price is the wrong comparison.
2. **Best-fit buyer: funded AI / SaaS / dev-tool startups and B2B product marketers.** They buy launches, demos and feature videos repeatedly, and some pay far above the norm ($80,000-$140,000 hype videos). Founders and heads of marketing decide (inference, confirm with early clients).
3. **Productize and sell monthly.** Real subscription prices: $1,100-$2,600/mo entry for video, $3,680 (Motion), $15,000 (Superside). A "launch kit + monthly changelog videos" plan at $1,500-$4,000/mo sits in the gap between design subscriptions and agency retainers (inference).
4. **Win on the bundle competitors charge extra for.** Others price 9:16/1:1/4:5, captions and cut-downs as add-ons ($150 per ratio, $30 captions). A code-driven pipeline can include all formats, SRT and 6/15/30 s cut-downs in every project at near-zero marginal cost.
5. **Sell speed.** Median studio production is 7 weeks; quotes run 4-6 weeks. A 3-7 day turnaround with a fast free sample (Motion offers a 10 s sample, 24-48 h) is a real edge.
6. **Do not compete with $11-per-video AI avatar ads or $68 logo gigs.** The floor collapsed. Compete where AI is weak: brand fidelity, accurate UI, clean typography, real data charts, story and script quality (Yum Yum, 2026-08-06).
7. **Avoid synthetic people.** 36% of consumers say AI video lowers brand trust; AI-generated people hurt most (Animoto, Jan 2026; VCU, second-hand). Vector motion graphics and real screen UI sidestep this. Be open about AI use in production.
8. **Ad-creative volume is the growth wedge.** Meta rewards creative diversity; advice ranges from 15-25 concepts to 50+ ads a month. Offer 10-20 vertical variants per month at $1,000-$3,500 per pack, priced by pack not hour.
9. **Fix the vendor complaints in the contract:** one round-by-round revision schedule (2-3 rounds), single decision-maker, a stated date, a named delivery list, editable source files as an optional paid add-on. Missed deadlines and endless revisions are the most reported pain, but the evidence is anecdotal.
10. **Price on outcomes and anchor high; the Bangladesh location is a cost edge, not a price signal.** Upwork's median for motion designers is $18-$35/hr, a floor to avoid. Quote in USD per project or retainer, show 2-3 case studies with a number (conversion, CTR), and target the US/EU/Gulf buyers who pay agency-level prices.
